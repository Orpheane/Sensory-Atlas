# Sensory Atlas - design principles

## Personalization, not universal judgment
There is no single experience of a public place. Describe its characteristics separately and let individuals decide what matters to them. Avoid labeling venues universally safe or unsafe.

## Show uncertainty and provenance
A future platform must distinguish:
- Personal experiences and subjective comments.
- Estimates aggregated from multiple observations.
- Instrument readings and how and when they were collected, if any.
- Missing, outdated, sparse or disputed information.

No real-world place claims are made by the current illustrative demo.

## Privacy by design
Browsing should not require a diagnosis, health information, or a public account. If location access is offered, request permission and support manual searches. Minimize personal data collection.

## Accessible by design
Offer keyboard operation, focus indicators, strong contrast, clear labels, reduced motion, and list-based alternatives to maps. A future product should work toward WCAG 2.2 AA and be tested with actual users.

## Community care
Crowdsourced information requires moderation, correction tools, contributor safety, and respectful handling of conflicting experiences. Avoid features that enable harassment or targeting sensitive locations.

## Honesty about project stage
This is an Ideathon concept showcase with fictional venues and ratings. It is not yet a real-world directory or live sensory safety service.
